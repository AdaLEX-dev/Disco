Title:
Post-Commune Executions and Wall of Martyrs

Text:
The aftermath of the crushed Revachol Commune in Year '08 was marked by systematic military terror, mass executions, and sweeping political purges conducted by victorious Coalition occupation forces and returning royalist officers. Determined to eradicate every trace of revolutionary ideology from the Insulindian population, military tribunals initiated summary trials that processed hundreds of captured communard soldiers, union organizers, Red Army commissars, and suspected civilian sympathizers each day. Neighborhood by neighborhood, suspected revolutionaries were rounded up and condemned without legal representation.

Execution squads operated continuously across the ruins of Revachol West. In Martinaise, the northern perimeter wall of the Feld Building served as a primary execution site, where thousands of communard fighters, textile workers, and local students were lined up and shot by firing squads before being cast into unmarked coastal trenches. The bullet pocked red bricks of the structure, stained with grease and powder burns, earned the somber designation of the Wall of Martyrs, becoming a silent memorial to the annihilated revolutionary generation.

The purges effectively wiped out the political and cultural vanguard of Revachol's working class. Surviving family members were subjected to forced labor, civic disenfranchisement, and relentless police surveillance under the newly established Coalition Administrative Region. The trauma of the massacres cast an enduring pall of grief, fear, and unspoken resentment over the working class districts of Jamrock, Coal City, and Martinaise, instilling a profound generational memory of communal martyrdom that modern municipal authorities, foreign corporate overseers, and occupying military garrisons have never completely extinguished.
