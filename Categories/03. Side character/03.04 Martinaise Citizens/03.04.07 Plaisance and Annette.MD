Title:
Plaisance and Annette

Text:
Plaisance and her young daughter Annette operate Crime, Romance and Biographies, a struggling independent commercial bookshop situated across the street from the Whirling-in-Rags in Martinaise. Plaisance is a fiercely conservative, moralistic, and anxious businesswoman who views the surrounding working class neighborhood with profound suspicion. Terrified of economic ruin, vagrancy, and municipal neglect, she manages her modest inventory of pulp fiction, religious commentaries, and regional histories with obsessive frugality. Her acute paranoia is intensified by rumors of a supernatural curse haunting the shuttered Doomed Commercial Area connected to the rear of her shop.

Despite the biting coastal chill and persistent rain, Plaisance forces her daughter Annette to stand outside on the pavement for hours distributing promotional leaflets to uninterested dockworkers and passersby. Annette is a remarkably bright, imaginative, and resilient child who copes with the monotony and cold by reading fantasy adventure books and inventing elaborate tabletop roleplaying scenarios in her head. While her mother obsessively calculates daily revenue and guards the building against suspected trespassers, Annette maintains an innocent curiosity about the eccentric visitors traversing the street.

Plaisance fiercely guards the heavy iron security door leading into the darkened labyrinth of the adjoining commercial building. Having witnessed decades of business failures and bankruptcies within the structure, she is convinced that an active spiritual malevolence destroys any commercial enterprise established on the premises. Her mix of petty bourgeois pride, parental harshness, and superstitious dread illustrates the deep psychological fragility of small business owners trying to survive in a decaying post industrial periphery.
