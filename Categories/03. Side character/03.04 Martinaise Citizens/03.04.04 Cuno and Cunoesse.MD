Title:
Cuno and Cunoesse

Text:
Kuuno de Ruyter, universally known as Cuno, and his feral companion Cunoesse are delinquent street youths occupying the alleyways and corrugated shack roofs behind the Whirling-in-Rags in Martinaise. Cuno is a hyperactive, volatile adolescent who masks severe domestic trauma behind a wall of manic, third person aggression, foul mouthed bravado, and relentless speed abuse. Living in squalor within the nearby Capeside apartments with an abusive, drug addicted former boxer father, Cuno survives on petty theft, scavenging, and sheer territorial defiance. He hurls obscenities and stones at passing municipal authorities, claiming absolute ownership over the ruined industrial backlots.

Perched above him on the roof of a discarded shipping shed lurks Cunoesse, a feral young girl whose hostility is even sharper and more venomous than Cuno's manic outbursts. Speaking with a harsh foreign cadence, she constantly goads Cuno into acts of escalating cruelty, suspicion, and paranoia, viewing any approaching adult as an existential threat. When an unidentified corpse is hung from a tree in the hostel courtyard, the two children turn the macabre scene into a grotesque playground, pelting the body with rocks while mocking the incompetence of the investigating militia officers.

Beneath Cuno's abrasive facade lies a remarkably observant and intelligent street survivor. Possessing acute situational awareness of coastal movements, hidden smuggling paths, and union activities, he demonstrates raw talent in industrial spray painting and acoustic design. When treated with firm professional authority rather than violence, Cuno reveals glimpses of vulnerability and a desperate desire for parental guidance, demonstrating the tragic waste of young human potential in the abandoned urban ruins of the Insulindian coastline.
