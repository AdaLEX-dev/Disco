Title:
Insulindian Coastal Gulls and Seabirds

Text:
The Insulindian Coastal Gulls and Seabirds comprise the vast, aggressive avian populations that dominate the skies, shorelines, and coastal waterways of the Bay of Revachol. Adapted to the punishing maritime meteorology of the southern archipelago, these resilient seabirds encompass massive grey-backed coastal gulls, storm petrels, and crested cormorants, whose raucous shrieks, beating wings, and scavenging habits form the ubiquitous acoustic background of everyday life across the port districts of Revachol West.

Equipped with heavy, oil saturated plumage that repels freezing ocean spray and dense coastal fog, these seabirds possess sharp, hooked beaks and powerful webbed feet adapted for seizing fish from breaking waves and clinging to sheer limestone cliffs. With the expansion of human industry and shipping commerce, the avian populations adapted with ruthless efficiency to urban scavenging. Flocks numbering in the thousands roost upon the rusting iron gantries of the Martinaise Commercial Harbour, the crumbling stone cornices of abandoned Dolorian basilicas, and the listing masts of anchored container barges, fiercely competing for discarded fish heads, slaughterhouse offal, and municipal waste.

To the human inhabitants of Revachol, coastal gulls represent an ambiguous cultural symbol. While fishermen and dockworkers frequently curse them as thieving pests that tear open cargo sacks and foul boat decks with white guano, they are also recognized as the indomitable, untamable spirit of the southern sea. Soaring effortlessly through violent autumn squalls and circling high above the ruins of naval bombardments, the coastal seabirds remain constant, watchful sentinels of the bay, thriving amidst the wreckage of human civilization with predatory grace and fierce biological vigor.
