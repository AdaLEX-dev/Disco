Title:
Coastal Crustaceans and Kelp Feeders

Text:
The Coastal Crustaceans and Kelp Feeders constitute the rich benthic invertebrate fauna and marine scavengers that populate the rocky shallows, tidal mudflats, and dense subaquatic kelp forests of the Bay of Revachol. Occupying the foundational tier of the regional maritime food web, these cold water invertebrates include armored king crabs, spiny mud lobsters, salt marsh prawns, and vast colonies of black kelp snails that graze upon the towering fronds of Insulindian giant kelp that carpet the seabed.

These marine organisms exhibit extraordinary physiological resilience, having adapted to survive not only the extreme hydrostatic pressures and cold saline temperatures of the southern bay, but also the heavy industrial runoff, coal soot, and chemical contaminants discharged by Revachol's coastal factories over the past century. Armored with thick, calcified carapaces that resist coastal predators and tidal wave action, crabs and bottom feeding shrimp scour the muddy seabed, consuming decaying biological debris, organic harbor waste, and sunken carrion, performing an indispensable natural filtration service that prevents the coastal waters from suffocating under urban pollution.

For the impoverished working class communities of Martinaise and the nearby fishing village, coastal crustaceans represent an essential, life sustaining dietary resource. Every morning at low tide, elderly scavengers, unemployed dockworkers, and shore foragers wade into the freezing mudflats with wire dip nets and baited wooden traps, harvesting buckets of shore crabs and mussels from rusted pier pilings and breakwaters. Steamed in seawater over makeshift stoves and seasoned with coarse vinegar, these humble marine creatures provide vital protein and sustenance to families struggling to survive on the margins of the Insulindian economy.
