Title:
Jamrock Urban Stray Dogs and Vermin

Text:
The Urban Stray Dogs and Vermin of Jamrock represent the feral domestic animal populations, opportunistic scavengers, and subterranean rodent colonies that have proliferated across the crumbling urban landscape of Revachol. Thriving in the ecological niche created by wartime destruction, decaying municipal sanitation systems, and pervasive human poverty, these resilient animals form a parallel feral society that mirrors the desperate survival struggles of the human populace inhabiting the surrounding tenement districts.

The most visible mammalian inhabitants of the streets are the packs of feral stray dogs that roam the alleys and bomb craters of Jamrock, Boogie Street, and the Valley of the Dogs. Descended from pre-revolutionary royalist guard hounds, industrial terriers, and abandoned family pets left behind during the panic of the Coalition invasion, these canines have reverted to tough, streetwise predators. Lean, scarred, and fiercely territorial, they survive by scavenging rotting market refuse, hunting street vermin, and defending neighborhood alleys from rival packs, occasionally forming wary, symbiotic truces with local vagrants and night watchmen who share scraps of chicory sausage in exchange for early warning against approaching patrol carriages.

Beneath the pavements, within the vast subterranean labyrinth of brick sewers and drainage conduits, dwell billions of urban sewer rats, wharf mice, and parasitic insects. Adapted to feast upon the unending waste of an unpaved metropolis, these vermin multiply in dark, flooded cellars and abandoned metro shafts. The stray dogs and urban vermin of Jamrock embody the harsh, unsentimental reality of post-war Revachol, proving that life adapts with fierce, primal tenacity amidst the ruin and neglect of an abandoned capital.
