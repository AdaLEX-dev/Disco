Title:
RCM Jurisdictional Authority and Protocols

Text:
The RCM Jurisdictional Authority and Protocols represent the pragmatic, often contradictory operating procedures governing the Revachol Citizens Militia as it enforces domestic peace across an occupied metropolis. Established by volunteer civilian brigades following the post-war collapse of municipal order, the RCM operates under a fragile occupational charter granted by the Coalition of Nations. This charter grants sworn militia officers legal authority to investigate homicides, apprehend violent felons, and regulate municipal street safety, while strictly denying them the status, legal immunities, and sovereign protections of a formal state police department.

To ensure the militia never develops the capacity to challenge foreign occupational garrisons, Coalition protocols impose severe operational constraints. RCM officers are explicitly forbidden from possessing military grade automatic weaponry, heavy tactical body armor, motorized gun carriages, or dedicated air support. Field operations rely heavily on decentralized precinct stations, standard issue service batons, civilian grade handguns, and the Coupris Kineema patrol carriage. Case investigations are formally recorded using the blue ledger system, a standardized paper documentation clipboard that tracks suspect testimonies, forensic evidence, and criminal charges.

In the neglected peripheral districts where formal civic institutions have ceased to function, RCM protocols mutate into a rough, survivalist doctrine of street negotiation and moral exhaustion. Officers must navigate a complex jurisdictional tightrope, securing informal permissions from militant labor syndicates and avoiding confrontations with foreign military police. Deprived of sovereign backing and institutional resources, the RCM sustains civic order through personal grit, psychological endurance, and an unwritten code of pragmatic compromise on the broken streets of Revachol.
