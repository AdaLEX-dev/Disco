Title:
Astra Brand Filterless Cigarettes

Text:
Astra Brand Filterless Cigarettes are the most ubiquitous, economically accessible tobacco product manufactured and consumed throughout the Insulindian Isola. Produced in vast commercial quantities by the Revachol State Tobacco Enterprise and later privatized under Coalition commercial licenses, Astra cigarettes are sold in simple rectangular blue-and-white paper soft packs adorned with a modest eight-pointed star emblem, available at every corner kiosk, tavern bar, and dockside market in the capital.

The blend consists of coarse, sun-cured dark tobacco leaves harvested in the agrarian provinces of Mundi and Semenine, cut roughly and rolled in thin, unbleached rice paper without a cellulose filter tip. The tobacco is characterized by its exceptionally high nicotine and tar content, emitting a pungent, acrid smoke smelling of burnt peat, dried chicory, and scorched molasses. When ignited by a sulfur friction match, an Astra cigarette burns hot and fast, providing an immediate, head-spinning nicotine rush that suppresses hunger, numbs fatigue, and cuts through the bitter coastal damp of West Revachol.

Across the social hierarchy of Revachol, Astra cigarettes function as a universal working-class currency and social lubricant. Dockworkers on the Debardeurs Union picket line, night-shift truck drivers on the Jamrock motorway, and weary RCM patrolmen huddled beneath rain-swept awnings constantly share and trade loose sticks from crumpled packs. Discarded Astra cigarette butts with their characteristic brown tobacco ends litter the boardwalks, puddles, and barroom floors of Martinaise, standing as an ever-present aromatic testament to the nervous tension, chemical dependency, and quiet endurance of the city's inhabitants through decades of foreign occupation and industrial decline.
