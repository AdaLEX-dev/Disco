Title:
Pre-Revolutionary Frissel Medals

Text:
Pre-Revolutionary Frissel Medals are ornate military and civilian decorations awarded by the royal crown of Revachol during the centuries of the Suzerainty prior to the Revolution of '02. Commissioned by royal decree and produced by master goldsmiths and engravers in Grand-Couron, these medals were bestowed upon imperial carabiniers, naval officers, colonial governors, and civic administrators who demonstrated distinguished service, martial valor, or unwavering loyalty to the Frissel royal dynasty.

The decorations are crafted from polished brass, gilded bronze, and fine vitreous enamel, suspended from silk moire ribbons dyed in the traditional royal livery colors of cobalt blue, gold, and crimson. Prominent awards include the Order of the Golden Sun, featuring a magnificent multi-pointed sunburst surrounding a raised relief profile of King Guillaume le Lion, and the Cross of Maritime Valor, awarded to marine soldiers who survived naval engagements in the Sea of Insulinde. The reverse of each medal is hand-engraved with the monarch's Latin motto, the recipient's military regiment, and the year of conferral.

Following the downfall of the monarchy and the declaration of the Commune, possession of royalist insignia was declared treasonous, and thousands of medals were confiscated, defaced, or melted down for scrap bullion. In the contemporary post-revolutionary era, surviving medals are held as sacred, tragic relics by elderly royalist veterans like Rene Arnoux, who proudly pins his tarnished decorations to his faded carabinier uniform while sitting by the petanque crater in Martinaise. These glittering metallic tokens stand as stubborn, defiant remnants of an aristocratic order drowned in blood and shell fire fifty years ago under the devastating guns of the Coalition fleet.
