Title:
The Isolas

Text:
An isola is a discreet terrestrial landmass enveloped by the connective medium of the pale, comprising habitable geography, bodies of liquid water, independent weather systems, and biological biomes. In the modern era, seven primary isolas have been surveyed, charted, and populated: Mundi, Seol, Graad, Samara, Katla, Iilmaraan, and Insulinde. Mundi stands as the oldest continually settled isola, long considered the cradle of civilized statehood and modern administrative law. Seol forms an isolated, technologically sovereign territory in the east characterized by advanced industrial isolation. Graad represents an expansive northern continental landmass historically governed by empires before undergoing violent communist revolution and economic transformation. Samara encompasses densely populated regions marked by longstanding socialist administration and bitter geopolitical rivalries. Katla remains known for geological activity, volcanic terrain, and distinct industrial extraction sectors. Iilmaraan possesses vast southern expanses and desert plateaus with ancient cultural lineages. Insulinde, discovered during the Dolorian century by Innocent Dolores Dei, is an oceanic isola characterized by archipelagos, massive maritime trade arteries, and the urban metropolis of Revachol. Physical communication and commerce between isolas cannot occur through conventional seafaring, as terrestrial oceans terminate at the boundary where liquid water dissolves into pale latitude. Transit between isolas is achieved through high-altitude aerostats travelling through charted coordinates within the pale. The size of an isola is measured not merely by terrestrial mileage but by the stability of its physical envelope against atmospheric degradation. Inhabitants of an isola share common atmospheric pressures, electromagnetic spectra, and biological fauna distinct from neighboring territories.
