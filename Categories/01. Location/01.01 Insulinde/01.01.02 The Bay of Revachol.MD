Title:
The Bay of Revachol

Text:
The Bay of Revachol is the deep natural maritime gulf situated along the northern coastline of the island landmass of Le Caillou within the Insulindian Isola. Enclosed by rugged rocky headlands and fed by the steady freshwater outflow of the Esperance River, the bay has served as the foundational naval and commercial anchorage for the city of Revachol across four centuries of continuous settlement. Its deep navigable channels and natural geological shelter from southern oceanic squalls allowed the Suzerainty of Revachol to establish the greatest drydocks, freight piers, and industrial cargo terminals known in the modern era.

The waters of the bay are cold, brackish, and heavily impacted by two centuries of intense industrial activity and maritime commerce. The shoreline is densely developed with municipal infrastructure, including the sprawling container storage yards of Terminal B, the passenger piers of Stella Maris, and the massive commercial wharves operated by the Debardeurs Union in Martinaise. Heavy concrete seawalls, iron mooring dolphins, and granite breakwaters line the coast, designed to resist the relentless tidal surges sweeping in from the Sea of Insulinde. Derelict iron hulls, ballast stone piles, and unexploded munitions from the Antecentennial War and the Coalition bombardment remain lodged deep within the silt of the outer harbor floor.

Ecologically, the Bay of Revachol sustains hardy coastal marine species adapted to heavily trafficked estuary conditions, including brackish kelp beds, blue rock crabs, and occasional migratory pods of Insulindian sea-bears. A permanent blanket of marine mist frequently cloaks the harbor during early morning hours, mingling with the coal smoke and diesel exhaust rising from the industrial quarters. Today, the bay is subject to the strict maritime jurisdiction of the Coalition of Nations, whose naval aerostats and gunboats monitor all shipping traffic.
