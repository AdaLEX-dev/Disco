Title:
Le Caillou

Text:
Le Caillou is the primary continental island of the Insulindian Isola and the geographic landmass upon which the metropolitan city of Revachol was constructed. Translated colloquially as the pebble or the rock, Le Caillou possesses a rugged topography characterized by coastal granite cliffs, rolling lowlands, and an interior spine of weathered hills. Its geographical position within the vast waters of the Sea of Insulinde rendered it the foundational territorial prize of the westward voyages organized during the Dolorian Century.

The natural environment of Le Caillou originally consisted of temperate deciduous forests, coastal salt marshes, and rocky estuaries. Following three centuries of relentless urbanization under the Frissel monarchy and subsequent industrial regimes, the natural terrain was largely buried beneath cobblestones, macadam avenues, rail corridors, and brick tenement blocks. Major municipal districts such as Jamrock, Old Town, Coal City, and Martinaise occupy its coastal lowlands, while older royal palaces and grand administrative complexes crown the higher inland ridges.

The island experiences a wet maritime climate shaped by the surrounding ocean and the encroaching pale. Cold winds sweep across the island from the west, driving heavy rainfall against the urban sprawl and producing prolonged winter chills without extensive snowfall. Soil quality outside the urban centers varies from rocky clay to marshland peat. While urban decay and wartime devastation have left vast tracts of the island scarred by craters and abandoned factory yards, Le Caillou remains the cultural and economic heart of the Insulindian Isola, housing millions of residents across its densely built coastal wards, central transit corridors, and decaying industrial sectors that border the wide estuary.
