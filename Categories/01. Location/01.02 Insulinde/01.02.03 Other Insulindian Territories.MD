Title:
Other Insulindian Territories

Text:
Beyond the metropolitan sprawl of Revachol, the isola of Insulinde encompasses diverse regional territories, coastal protectorates, and maritime dependencies spread across its expansive ocean basin. Notable among these territories are the rural hinterlands of Le Caillou, which feature agricultural valleys, timber estates, and decaying industrial infrastructure established during the antecentennial expansion. Across the waters of the Bay of Esperance lie satellite settlements such as Ozonne, Face-a-la-Mer, and the coastal communities of Grand-Couron, each maintaining distinct municipal identities shaped by fishing, maritime transport, and light manufacturing. To the east and south, regional protectorates originally surveyed by Sur-la-Clef and Oranjese mercantile companies operate under varying degrees of administrative autonomy, exporting citrus, minerals, and refined fuels to Revacholian docks. These territories were deeply affected by the Insulindian Civil War, during which revolutionary airship brigades fought Coalition naval forces across open straits and island harbors. In the modern post-war era, many secondary Insulindian settlements endure economic stagnation, their local industries dismantled or monopolized by foreign conglomerates like the Wild Pines Group. Transport between these territories relies on coastal ferries, motorized freighters, and regional road networks that wind through coastal bluffs and salt flats, connecting remote fishing villages and industrial depots to the central logistical arteries of the isola. Archival surveys preserved in municipal collections confirm these topographical and administrative markers across all recorded eras. Navigational authorities maintain precise records detailing physical access corridors, seasonal variations, and regional border demarcations.
