Title:
Insulindian Waters

Text:
The Insulindian ocean basin comprises an expansive body of cold, nutrient-rich saltwater that surrounds the landmasses of Le Caillou and neighboring archipelagos before merging into the southern pale. Its marine hydrology is driven by deep oceanic trenches and the Esperance Current, which sweeps past the coastal shelf of Revachol, regulating regional air temperatures and bringing heavy cloud cover and seasonal rainstorms. Historically, these waters supported vast fishing fleets harvesting herring, cod, and localized marine crustaceans that fed the expanding populations of the industrial revolution. The floor of the bay and coastal straits contains extensive geological deposits of sedimentary silt alongside centuries of sunken naval wreckage resulting from colonial engagements, pirate skirmishes, and the aerial-naval battles of the Coalition intervention. Marine pollution resulting from decades of untreated municipal runoff, chemical effluent from petrochemical terminals, and industrial dredging has severely degraded near-shore aquatic life, particularly around Martinaise and the harbor approaches. Despite ecological stress, the outer waters maintain significant commercial value as international shipping channels through which deep-draft cargo carriers, oil tankers, and bulk freighters enter the port facilities of Revachol under international Coalition naval patrol. Navigational authorities maintain precise records detailing physical access corridors, seasonal variations, and regional border demarcations.
