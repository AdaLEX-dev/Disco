Title:
Old Town

Text:
Old Town is the oldest continuously inhabited urban quarter of Revachol, situated on the rocky coastal hills rising immediately south of the historic harbor on the island of Le Caillou. Founded during the early decades following the westward voyages of Innocence Dolores Dei, Old Town retains the medieval and early modern architectural footprint of the original colonial settlement that preceded the grand imperial expansions of the Frissel monarchy.

The district is defined by narrow, winding cobblestone lanes, steep stone stairways, and tightly packed multi-story timber and masonry dwellings with steep gabled roofs and overhanging upper floors. Ancient stone wayside shrines dedicated to Dolores Dei and early Dolorian saints stand at street intersections, their carved alcoves often adorned with melted wax candles and withered coastal flowers placed by pious neighborhood residents. Small public squares paved with uneven river stones host traditional morning markets where fishwives, vegetable vendors, and antique merchants trade local produce and salvaged domestic heirlooms beneath the shadow of historic stone bell towers.

In the contemporary era, Old Town functions as a quiet, atmospheric residential enclave somewhat insulated from the massive industrial scale of the neighboring port quarters. Its inhabitants include generational Revacholian families, artisans, antique bookbinders, and elderly retirees who maintain traditional Insulindian customs, culinary recipes, and local dialects. Because its dense layout of narrow alleys and steep inclines prevents modern Kineema motor carriage traffic, pedestrians and delivery handcarts dominate the thoroughfares, preserving a distinct sense of historical permanence amid the widespread urban devastation that characterizes the post-revolutionary metropolis.
