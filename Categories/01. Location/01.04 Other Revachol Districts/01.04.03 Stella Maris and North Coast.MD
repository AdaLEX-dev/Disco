Title:
Stella Maris and North Coast

Text:
Stella Maris is a prominent maritime coastal district occupying the northern headlands of Le Caillou, overlooking the outer shipping channels of the Bay of Revachol in the Insulindian Isola. Developed during the late nineteenth century as the municipal gateway for international passenger navigation and maritime customs administration, the district combines grand transport terminals with upscale residential enclaves overlooking the cold expanse of the bay.

The district waterfront is anchored by the Stella Maris Passenger Piers, an extensive complex of stone docks, cast-iron transit sheds, and art-deco passenger terminals where interisolary ocean liners and coastal ferry services historically disembarked hundreds of thousands of travelers. Prominent government buildings, including the former Admiralty Bureau and the Maritime Quarantine Station, line the seaside esplanades, their sturdy granite facades designed to withstand the violent northerly gales that sweep in from the Sea of Insulinde. Inland from the docks, terraced villas and leafy avenues house affluent shipping executives, naval architects, and consular officials.

Following the Antecentennial War and the subsequent establishment of the Coalition military administration, Stella Maris retained a greater degree of economic stability and public order than the devastated industrial wards of West Revachol. The passenger piers continue to service commercial ferry traffic linking Le Caillou with outlying island communities across the isola, while Coalition naval launches utilize the deep-water moorings for coastal patrol missions. The rocky northern beaches, bounded by granite seawalls and maritime navigation beacons, offer sweeping panoramic views of the outer harbor and the cold, grey horizon where the ocean merges with the pale.
