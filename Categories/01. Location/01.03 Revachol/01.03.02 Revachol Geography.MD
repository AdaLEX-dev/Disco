Title:
Revachol Geography

Text:
The physical topography of Revachol is defined by a natural amphitheater of undulating limestone ridges and coastal bluffs rising from the northern shore of the Bay of Revachol. The city is bisected from north to south by the Esperance River, a wide, slow-moving waterway whose delta channels and alluvial deposits historically determined early urban settlement patterns. To the east and northeast, the elevation rises toward the bluffs of Grand-Couron and the inland industrial plateaus, while the western perimeter slopes downward into low-lying marshlands, coastal estuaries, and the tidal salt flats of Martinaise. Decades of intensive urban expansion have radically altered the natural hydrology of the area, as smaller tributaries were diverted into subterranean masonry culverts or enclosed concrete storm drains. Soil composition across the metropolitan core consists of heavy clay intermixed with gravel and centuries of compacted rubble resulting from urban rebuilding programs. Seismic activity is minimal, but the maritime atmosphere introduces high humidity, dense coastal fog banks, and persistent winter precipitation that blankets the cobblestone roads in slush and freezing drizzle. The city natural shoreline has been extensively transformed through land reclamation, stone breakwaters, and timber pier systems engineered to accommodate commercial vessels of significant tonnage.
