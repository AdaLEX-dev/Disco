Title:
Revachol Inland Areas

Text:
The inland zones of Revachol extend northward from the coastal basin into undulating hills, agricultural river flats, and former industrial manufacturing corridors. These areas encompass outlying residential communes, freight rail marshaling yards, and expansive storage depots that historically supplied the urban core with raw materials, livestock, and grain. Prominent among inland areas are the neighborhoods bordering the River Esperance, where brick warehouses, textile mills, and chemical processing plants stand adjacent to worker housing developments. As elevation increases toward the north, the cityscape yields to semi-rural suburbs, market gardens, and fragmented tracts of temperate pine and birch forest that cover the interior ridges of Le Caillou. Inland transportation relies on secondary paved highways and heavy rail lines that connect Revachol to satellite industrial towns across the island. Decades of post-war deindustrialization have left many inland factories shuttered, their corrugated steel roofs rusted through and overgrown with weeds, while local populations subsist through informal economies, scrap salvage, and small-scale municipal transit operations. Despite this economic contraction, the inland corridors remain critical for commercial logistics, providing trucking routes that bypass congested coastal streets. Navigational authorities maintain precise records detailing physical access corridors, seasonal variations, and regional border demarcations.
