Title:
Revachol Architecture

Text:
Architectural heritage across Revachol presents a dense physical timeline of historical styles, ranging from pre-revolutionary royalist grandeur to utilitarian communist modularity and post-war emergency reconstruction. The reign of King Guillaume the Millionth introduced expansive Dolorian neoclassicism, characterized by symmetrical limestone facades, grand colonnades, carved stone cornices, and gilded bronze ornamentation adorning municipal palaces, opera houses, and military academies. Following the overthrow of the suzerainty, the revolutionary Commune pioneered early modern structural utilitarianism, utilizing reinforced concrete, exposed steel framing, and modular multi-story apartment complexes designed to house large worker collectives efficiently. The violent suppression of the Commune by Coalition artillery left extensive physical scars across the urban landscape, creating vast zones where shattered neoclassical stonework was roughly repaired with corrugated sheet metal, cinder blocks, and salvaged timber. In the contemporary era, architectural development is split between high-modern glass-and-steel towers constructed by international banking corporations in Central Revachol and decaying historic tenements across West Revachol where damp, peeling plaster, exposed wiring, and rusted iron fire escapes dominate the residential streetscape.
