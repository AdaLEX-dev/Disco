Title:
Revachol Districts

Text:
The metropolitan extent of Revachol is divided into numerous distinct municipal districts, quarters, and administrative zones that reflect distinct historical origins, socioeconomic classes, and architectural styles. West Revachol encompasses dense, historically proletarian districts including Jamrock, Martinaise, Coal City, and the Old Town, which bore the brunt of revolutionary fighting and subsequent Coalition shelling. Jamrock represents an immense, densely populated urban quarter characterized by narrow tenements, bustling street markets, and high concentrations of working-class families. Central Revachol contains the administrative, judicial, and financial centers of the city, where neoclassical stone boulevards and corporate office headquarters house foreign banks, consular delegations, and the offices of the Moralintern. East Revachol and Grand-Couron feature broad avenues, historic aristocratic manors, landscaped parks, and upper-middle-class residential developments that largely escaped the physical devastation of the war. To the south, along the harbor perimeters, industrial terminals, container yards, and automated crane complexes handle the continuous inflow of international cargo. Each district operates under distinct demographic realities, with municipal public services, road paving, electrical grid reliability, and police patrol coverage varying dramatically between the affluent quarters and the neglected western slums. Archival surveys preserved in municipal collections confirm these topographical and administrative markers across all recorded eras. Navigational authorities maintain precise records detailing physical access corridors, seasonal variations, and regional border demarcations. Strategic assessments produced by regional planners continue to emphasize the enduring material importance of this sector across the isola.
