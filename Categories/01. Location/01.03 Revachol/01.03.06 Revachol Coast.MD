Title:
Revachol Coast

Text:
The coastal margin of Revachol extends along the northern waters of the Bay of Revachol, spanning miles of industrial wharves, tidal mudflats, stone seawalls, and rocky capes. In the west, the shoreline is low and marshy, featuring salt-encrusted wooden boardwalks, decaying fishing shacks, and tidal channels where small motor launches navigate shallow mudbanks. As the coast moves eastward toward the central and harbor districts, the coastline has been heavily reinforced with massive cut-stone sea walls, dry docks, and deep-water berths capable of docking ocean-going container freighters and military vessels. The marine environment along the urban perimeter is subject to strong tidal fluctuations, exposing wide expanses of black harbor silt, rusted metal debris, and industrial slag at low tide. Sea breezes carry the smell of brine, diesel oil, and rotting seaweed across coastal neighborhoods, often accompanied by dense maritime fogs that obscure navigation lights and coastal watchtowers. Historic fortifications, coastal gun emplacements, and masonry lighthouses dating from the royalist era remain perched on high promontories overlooking the bay, many standing abandoned or repurposed as civilian storehouses following the demilitarization of the city by Coalition forces.
