Title:
Martinaise Geography

Text:
Geographically, Martinaise occupies a low-lying coastal peninsula bounded by the saltwater inlet of the bay to the south and west and separated from the main urban body of Jamrock by a deep canal channel to the east. The terrain consists of damp coastal sand, heavy marine clay, and reclaimed tidal flats that remain vulnerable to flooding during high tides and autumn gales. The western portion of the district narrows into a windswept sandspit known as Land End, where desolate salt marshes, reed beds, and rocky tidal shoals face the open sea. Inland, the ground rises gently toward the elevated concrete foundations of the industrial harbor and the abandoned railway embankments that once carried heavy freight across the isola. Natural drainage is poor, resulting in persistent standing puddles, brackish water pools, and muddy vacant lots where rubble from wartime shelling has settled into the earth. The regional microclimate is maritime and severe, characterized by cold onshore winds, persistent grey cloud cover, and frequent freezing rain that accelerates the oxidation of exposed metal beams and the rot of unpainted wooden structures across the waterfront. Archival surveys preserved in municipal collections confirm these topographical and administrative markers across all recorded eras. Navigational authorities maintain precise records detailing physical access corridors, seasonal variations, and regional border demarcations.
