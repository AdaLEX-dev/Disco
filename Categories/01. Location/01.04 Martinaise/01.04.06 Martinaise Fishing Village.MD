Title:
Martinaise Fishing Village

Text:
The Martinaise Fishing Village is a small, impoverished settlement situated on the marshy coastal flats southwest of the main district center, accessible via a dilapidated wooden footbridge. The village consists of approximately a dozen haphazardly constructed timber shacks, raised on stilts to protect them from regular tidal surges and storm swells rolling off the bay. Inhabited by a resilient community of net pickers, generational fishermen, and informal squatters, the settlement operates largely outside municipal oversight, lacking paved streets, municipal running water, or a connection to the city power grid. Residents rely on traditional wooden skiffs, hand-woven gill nets, and fish traps to harvest localized populations of flounder, sea snails, and shoreline crabs that endure in the bay waters. Drying racks, piles of rusted engine parts, tangled synthetic nets, and heaps of discarded mussel shells surround each dwelling. Despite extreme poverty and structural isolation, the village maintains a close-knit communal ethos, sharing communal wood-burning stoves and providing mutual assistance against the seasonal gales that threaten to sweep the fragile shacks into the sea.
