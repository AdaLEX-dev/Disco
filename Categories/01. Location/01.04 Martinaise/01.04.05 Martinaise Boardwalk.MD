Title:
Martinaise Boardwalk

Text:
The Martinaise Boardwalk is an elevated wooden promenade that curves along the western shoreline of the district, originally erected during the late royalist era as a scenic pedestrian concourse for wealthy vacationers. Constructed from heavy creosote-treated timber planks mounted on sunken stone footings, the boardwalk has deteriorated through decades of exposure to salt spray and lack of municipal maintenance, resulting in splintered deck boards, rusted iron guardrails, and missing sections patched with scrap plywood. Along its landward side stand shuttered ticket booths, decaying seaside cabanas, and the faded pastel stucco walls of former souvenir shops that have remained vacant since the revolution. Despite its ruined state, the boardwalk remains a vital pedestrian route for residents traveling between the central roundabout and the coastal fishing shacks to the west. Local youth congregate along its sheltered alcoves, while elderly residents pause to watch the grey oceanic swells roll in against the sea wall. Strong westerly winds frequently carry cold ocean spray across the planks, coating the wooden railings in a thin glaze of sea salt and frost during winter mornings.
