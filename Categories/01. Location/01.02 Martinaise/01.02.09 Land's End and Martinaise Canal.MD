Title:
Land's End and Martinaise Canal

Text:
Land's End is the wild, rocky coastal headland marking the westernmost geographic boundary of the Martinaise district, flanked by the artificial waterway known as the Martinaise Canal. Constructed during the municipal engineering projects of the Suzerainty of Revachol, the canal cuts inland through coastal marshland, linking the Bay of Revachol to the industrial drainage channels of West Revachol and providing a controlled navigable corridor for timber and gravel barges.

The junction between the canal and the open bay is regulated by a heavy cast-iron lock gate and hydraulic control bridge. Due to the total breakdown of municipal maintenance following the Coalition intervention of '08, the mechanical gears, counterbalance chains, and hydraulic pistons have suffered severe corrosion. The movable bridge mechanism frequently jams, cutting off vehicle and pedestrian transit between Martinaise and the Capeside coastal highway for days or weeks at a time until manually forced with winches or prybars. Stagnant green canal water laps against slime-coated concrete retaining walls, accumulating petroleum slicks, industrial plastics, and discarded maritime packaging.

Beyond the canal locks lies the desolate coastal strip of Land's End, defined by jagged granite reefs, wind-stunted reeds, and coastal salt scrub. The area is heavily littered with maritime drift, rusted engine parts, and the skeletal remnants of wooden rowboats abandoned on the rocks. A crumbling concrete sea wall, scarred by artillery impact craters from Coalition naval gunnery, lines the shore, offering an unhindered vantage over the open waters of the bay toward the abandoned Sea Fortress Island and the distant oceanic pale horizon. Lone beachcombers and fishermen occasionally brave the freezing coastal spray to gather firewood or salvage scrap from the low-tide gravel beds.
