Title:
The Martinaise Fishing Village

Text:
The Martinaise Fishing Village is an informal, impoverished coastal settlement situated on the marshy tidal mudflats and rocky shoreline north-west of the main urban quarter of Martinaise. Originally settled by independent net-menders and seasonal seafood harvesters during the expansion of the commercial harbor, the village evolved into a permanent shantytown populated by working-class families, destitute dockers, and elderly maritime laborers displaced by industrial mechanization and wartime destruction.

The architecture of the settlement is entirely vernacular and improvised. Dwellings consist of ramshackle wooden shacks built upon timber stilts to elevate living quarters above routine tidal surges. Structures are constructed from discarded packing crates, corrugated sheet iron, driftwood, and tar-coated canvas, insulated against the bitter coastal damp with layers of old newsprint and wool rags. Narrow timber walkways and slippery plank ramps connect the dispersed cabins, spanning stagnant tidal pools, rotting kelp beds, and brackish drainage ditches littered with discarded tin cans and broken glass.

Lacking municipal electricity, running water, or sanitary sewer connections, the village relies on wood-burning cast-iron stoves and kerosene lamps for heat and illumination. The residents survive through small-scale subsistence fishing, harvesting blue rock crabs and coastal mollusks from the bay, and mending synthetic gill nets for commercial trawlers operating out of Terminal B. Communal laundry lines stretch between the stilts, where washerwomen beat heavy woolens in the freezing salt air. Despite its extreme material deprivation and harsh exposure to sub-zero oceanic gales, the settlement retains a tight-knit communal solidarity, offering shelter to vagrants, fugitives, and forgotten veterans of the Antecentennial War.
