Title:
The Capeside Apartments

Text:
The Capeside Apartments are a sprawling residential tenement housing block situated across the Martinaise Canal along the western coastal fringe of the district. Erected during the industrial expansion of the early twentieth century to house growing numbers of debardeurs, factory laborers, and transport mechanics, the multi-story concrete and brick structure represents a typical example of utilitarian working-class housing constructed across Revachol West.

The tenement features long, dim central hallways lined with identical wooden apartment doors, illuminated by flickering low-wattage electric bulbs and ventilated by narrow air shafts that carry the pervasive odors of boiled turnip soup, chicory grounds, drying laundry, and damp floorboards. The plaster walls are cracked and stained by coastal moisture, and the building's exterior stucco has peeled away to expose bare cinderblocks and rusted rebar to the persistent salt winds sweeping in from the bay. The communal washrooms and unheated stairwells bear witness to decades of minimal maintenance, leaking copper plumbing, and chronic municipal neglect.

The residents of Capeside comprise an eclectic cross-section of Revacholian society surviving on the margins of the formal economy. Inhabitants include elderly pensioners who lived through the Antecentennial War, night-shift harbor janitors, cleaning ladies, dockworkers, and itinerant laborers renting shared rooms on weekly leases. An exterior metal fire escape and concrete balconies overlook the canal and the rear industrial railyards, providing a cold gathering spot where tenants smoke Astra cigarettes, lean against rusted railings, and observe the distant shipping traffic maneuvering through the fog-shrouded harbor beneath the humming Coalition aerostats on freezing evenings.
