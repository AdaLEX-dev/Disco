Title:
The Church of the Dolorian

Text:
The Church of the Dolorian is an ancient, monumental stone cathedral situated on the windswept coastal spit south of the Martinaise fishing settlement overlooking the Bay of Revachol. Constructed nearly four centuries ago during the early colonial settlement of Le Caillou, the cathedral was consecrated to Innocence Dolores Dei, whose historical westward expedition brought the Insulindian Isola into the religious and political fold of the Holy Dolorian Church.

Architecturally, the building represents an imposing example of early Perikarnasian ecclesiastical stonework. It features soaring granite archways, heavy buttresses, and an immense stained glass window positioned high in the eastern nave depicting Innocence Dolores Dei with her arms outstretched and her luminous lungs illuminated by the morning sun. The cavernous interior nave is topped by complex wooden rafters and an elevated timber suspension network originally constructed to support liturgical choral apparatus and hanging votive lanterns. Weathered stone frescoes depicting historical martyrs and early missionary saints line the side aisles, their painted features faded by decades of rising damp.

Following the anti-clerical campaigns of the Revolution of '02 and the devastation of the Coalition bombardment, the cathedral was deconsecrated, abandoned, and left open to the elements. Decades of coastal gales have shattered sections of the stained glass and weakened the timber flooring, allowing sea winds to howl through the nave. In the center of the sanctuary lies a subtle acoustic and sensory anomaly, an unnatural silence linked by local folklore to the encroaching influence of the pale. In recent years, the derelict building has been adopted as an informal squat by youth subcultures experimenting with high-output anodic dance music equipment and tape machines.
