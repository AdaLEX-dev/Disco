Title:
Revacholian Colonial Demographics and Migrations

Text:
The Revacholian Colonial Demographics and Migrations trace the demographic transformation of the Insulindian Isola from an unsettled southern archipelago into the most densely populated, polyglot imperial metropolis in Elysium. The initial foundation of the modern demographic landscape commenced with the landing of Innocence Dolores Dei over three hundred years ago, which introduced tens of thousands of western colonial pioneers, military garrisons, and Dolorian administrative bureaucrats from the isola of Mundi to the rocky promontory of Le Caillou.

During the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, the rapid industrialization of Revachol under the Frissel monarchy sparked immense trans-isolary labor migrations. Hundreds of thousands of indentured stevedores, skilled metalworkers, and agricultural laborers migrated from across the globe, including Semenese plantation hands, Graadian railway technicians, and Seolite commercial clerks. These diverse populations settled across specialized urban districts: commercial shipping communities colonized Martinaise and the harbor perimeter, heavy industrial proletarians concentrated in Coal City and Jamrock, and wealthy merchant elites established palatial estates on Le Caillou and Grand-Couron.

This vast demographic mosaic was cataclysmically altered by the historical violence of the twentieth century. The bloody suppression of the Revachol Commune and the devastating Coalition naval bombardment of Year '08 resulted in the death or displacement of hundreds of thousands of working-class citizens, leaving entire neighborhoods depopulated and shattered. In contemporary Revachol, surviving colonial descendants navigate an uneasy coexistence with foreign Coalition occupational garrisons, forming an impoverished, resilient urban populace that reflects centuries of global migration, colonial exploitation, and revolutionary tragedy.
